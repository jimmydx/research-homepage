About

I study atmospheric gravity and acoustic waves and their role in coupling the lower atmosphere, upper atmosphere, and ionosphere.

I am a Research Scientist in Embry-Riddle’s Center for Space and Atmospheric Research (CSAR).

Research expertise

The work combines numerical simulations with satellite observations, ground-based airglow imaging, and Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) total electron content (TEC) measurements. Geodetic transformations, spectral analysis, and synthetic observations support comparisons across instruments, spatial scales, and atmospheric regions.

Research methods and datasets

AWE · Atmospheric Waves Experiment

As principal investigator for Embry-Riddle’s student collaboration on NASA’s Atmospheric Waves Experiment (AWE), I guide student work on numerical simulations and comparisons between AWE observations and GNSS TEC measurements.

The studies examine gravity waves associated with convection and compare mesospheric observations with ionospheric disturbances. Weather observations and atmospheric models provide information on the sources and propagation conditions.

AWE visualization gallery at USU

MITIGATE

I serve as project manager for MITIGATE, an Office of Naval Research (ONR) MURI project involving Embry-Riddle, Arizona State, Boise State, Boston University, and the University of Miami.

The project combines observations and atmospheric and ionospheric modeling to study gravity waves generated by severe weather. Its objectives are to characterize their propagation and ionospheric effects and improve forecasts of disturbances affecting radio communication and navigation.

MITIGATE announcement

AtmoSense / AIRWaveS

I previously served as project manager for AIRWaveS, part of DARPA’s Atmosphere as a Sensor (AtmoSense) program.

AtmoSense investigated whether atmospheric and ionospheric signatures could be used to detect and characterize transient events such as earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, and storms. AIRWaveS used source-to-sensor numerical simulations and field measurements to assess signal detectability and compare modeled and observed responses.

Research & visualization

Studies of wave sources, propagation, and atmospheric and ionospheric responses using observations and numerical models.

Wave sources, modeling, and analysis

Sources and atmospheric coupling

Studies examine gravity waves generated by convection and topography, their propagation, and the associated upper-atmospheric and ionospheric responses. Weather observations, atmospheric background conditions, and wave measurements are compared to identify tropospheric sources and assess propagation. Related work examines acoustic waves generated by earthquakes and their signatures in airglow and TEC measurements.

Numerical simulations and observational comparisons

Numerical simulations are compared with AWE and Atmospheric Infrared Sounder (AIRS) observations, ground-based airglow images, and GNSS TEC measurements. Synthetic observations account for viewing geometry, line-of-sight integration, and instrument sensitivity when comparing modeled fields with measurements. This work also draws on experience with satellite orbit determination and attitude analysis.

Characterization of atmospheric gravity waves

Geodetic transformations place observations and model output in consistent coordinate systems. Spectral analyses estimate wave periods, wavelengths, and propagation directions. Comparisons across instruments, spatial and temporal scales, and atmospheric regions are used to examine source associations and propagation. ERA5 and MERRA-2 reanalyses, along with NAVGEM and WACCM-X model products, provide background conditions for interpreting wave propagation.

3D visualization and extended reality

3D visualizations show observations and simulation output at their geographic locations and altitudes. They are used to inspect wave structure, compare datasets, and present results.

Extended reality (XR)

I work with 3D creation software and real-time engines, including Blender, Unity, and Unreal Engine, to prepare data, edit scenes, and distribute them across platforms. Scenes contain georeferenced observations and volumetric simulation output. XR experience includes viewing and interacting with scientific data on Apple Vision Pro and augmented reality headsets.

Student mentoring

Undergraduate training in scientific programming, data analysis, and 3D visualization.

Mentoring covers MATLAB, Python, and Blender for atmospheric research. Students work with observations and simulation output, apply spectral analyses, and prepare 3D visualizations of atmospheric structure.

I help students develop analysis code, interpret results, and document the assumptions behind calculations and visualizations. Projects emphasize comparisons between atmospheric layers and between simulated and observed quantities.
